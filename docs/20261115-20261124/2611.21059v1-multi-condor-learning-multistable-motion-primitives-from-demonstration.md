## 摘要
基于动力系统的运动策略为学习具有稳定性和收敛性的反应式机器人运动提供了框架。然而，许多操作任务存在若干不同目标或一组连续的可接受结果，因此需要能够保留这些备选方案的运动基元。在具有连续自主动力学的统一框架中同时兼顾这两类目标结构仍是一个开放挑战。我们提出一种基于 CONDOR 的框架 multi-CONDOR，用于学习具有多个离散吸引子或连续吸引目标轮廓的运动基元。该方法将模仿学习与损失函数相结合，这些损失鼓励学习到的任务动力学与编码所需吸引结构的潜在参考系统之间保持一致。我们给出了关于所学运动动力学收敛性质的基于 Lyapunov 的分析。我们在具有离散目标的仿真基准、具有连续目标轮廓的定性示例以及机器人抓取实验中评估了该框架。在仿真中，与由状态机选择的分别学习的单目标运动基元相比，multi-CONDOR 将轨迹 RMSE 降低了约 72%。对于分别具有两个和三个目标任务，它将评估初始状态中最终落在目标容差之外的比例从 StableNODE 的 0.31% 和 7.29% 降至两种设置下的 0%。使用 Franka Emika 机械臂的实验表明，利用从示范中学习到的运动，能够通过容器的两个把手之一成功抓取该容器。这些结果共同表明，multi-CONDOR 将运动复现与收敛到其他有效结果的灵活性结合起来。

## Abstract
Dynamical-system-based motion policies provide a framework for learning reactive robot motions with stability and convergence properties. Many manipulation tasks, however, admit several distinct goals or a continuous set of acceptable outcomes, requiring motion primitives that preserve these alternatives. Accommodating both goal structures within a unified framework with continuous, autonomous dynamics remains an open challenge. We propose a CONDOR-based framework, multi-CONDOR, for learning motion primitives with either multiple discrete attractors or a continuous attracting goal contour. The approach combines imitation learning with losses that encourage agreement between the learned task dynamics and latent reference systems encoding the desired attraction structure. We provide a Lyapunov-based analysis of the convergence properties of the learned motion dynamics. We evaluate the framework on simulated benchmarks with discrete goals, qualitative examples with continuous goal contours, and robotic grasping experiments. In simulation, multi-CONDOR reduces trajectory RMSE by approximately 72% compared with separately learned single-goal motion primitives selected by a state machine. For tasks with two and three goals, respectively, it reduces the fraction of evaluated initial states ending outside the goal tolerance from StableNODE's 0.31% and 7.29% to 0% in both settings. Experiments with a Franka Emika robot arm demonstrate successful grasping of a container by either of its two handles using motions learned from demonstrations. Together, these results show multi-CONDOR combines motion reproduction with the flexibility to converge to alternative valid outcomes.
