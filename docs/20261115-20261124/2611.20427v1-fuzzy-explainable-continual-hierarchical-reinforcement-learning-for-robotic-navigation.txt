Fuzzy Explainable Continual Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning
for Robotic Navigation
Peng Qin, Tao Zhao,
Abstract—Continual robot navigation requires reinforcement
learning policies to continuously adapt to new environments while
retaining previously acquired knowledge. Meanwhile, explainable
reinforcement learning aims to learn meaningful behavioral rep-
resentations through policy interaction. However, these objectives
are inherently conflicting, as continual learning emphasizes policy
stability whereas explainable behavior learning requires adaptive
exploration. Existing methods rarely address both simultaneously.
To resolve this issue, this paper proposes a Fuzzy Explain-
able Continual Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (FECHRL)
method. A two-stage behavior cloning strategy is introduced
to balance rapid policy adaptation and explainable behavior
evolution by progressively transitioning from strong imitation
guidance to autonomous reinforcement learning. Furthermore, a
fuzzy critic based on a Takagi–Sugeno fuzzy inference system
is proposed to alleviate value extrapolation errors during the
transition between imitation-guided learning and autonomous
exploration, thereby improving continual training stability. Ex-
perimental results on sequential multi-scenario navigation tasks
demonstrate that the proposed method effectively unifies contin-
ual learning capability and policy explainability within a single
reinforcement learning framework, achieving stable knowledge
retention and meaningful behavioral explanation.
Index Terms—Continual reinforcement learning; Explainabil-
ity; Fuzzy critic; Hierarchical reinforcement learning; Robotic
navigation
I. INTRODUCTION
N
Avigation is one of the fundamental capabilities required
for autonomous robots operating in complex environ-
ments, with broad applications in transportation, inspection,
and service robotics. In recent years, reinforcement learning
(RL) has demonstrated strong decision-making capability in
end-to-end navigation tasks. However, robotic navigation in
continual multi-environment scenarios still faces several im-
portant challenges.
First, when learning new navigation tasks, RL often requires
extensive interaction and random exploration, resulting in low
learning efficiency and unstable policy optimization in com-
plex environments. Although prior knowledge and behavior
cloning (BC) can alleviate this issue by accelerating policy
initialization, excessive dependence on cloning guidance may
constrain policy adaptability and reduce the capability of
autonomous behavior learning. Second, in continual learning
(CL) scenarios, adapting to new environments often causes
catastrophic forgetting of previously learned navigation skills,
thereby degrading long-term task performance. Furthermore,
most existing RL-based navigation methods rely on black-
box policy representations, making it difficult to provide

explainability for navigation behaviors and internal decision-
making processes.
Existing studies have attempted to address these problems
from different perspectives. Continual reinforcement learning
(CRL) methods alleviate forgetting through parameter regular-
ization and memory replay mechanisms [1], [2], while explain-
able RL methods improve policy explainability through hier-
archical structures, latent behavior representations, or reward
decomposition [3], [4]. In addition, BC and imitation learning
have been widely used to accelerate policy learning and reduce
negative transfer during early-stage training. However, these
approaches are typically studied independently. In particular,
CL and explainability-oriented policy learning exhibit conflict-
ing requirements. CL prefers stronger prior constraints to sta-
bilize policy transfer, whereas explainability-oriented behavior
learning requires sufficient autonomous interaction to form
environment-adaptive and semantically meaningful behavior
patterns. Excessive cloning guidance may therefore suppress
the emergence of explainable behavior representations.
To address this issue, this study proposes an Explainable
Fuzzy-Enhanced Continual Hierarchical Reinforcement Learn-
ing method, which jointly considers CL capability and policy
explainability within a unified navigation framework. To bal-
ance knowledge guidance and autonomous behavior formation,
a two-stage behavior cloning mechanism is introduced. During
the early training stage, strong cloning supervision is employed
to accelerate policy acquisition and alleviate negative transfer,
while in the later stage the cloning constraint is weakened to
release exploration capability and enable the formation of ex-
plainable behavior patterns through environmental interaction.
Furthermore, to stabilize the transition from cloning-guided
learning to autonomous interaction learning, a fuzzy critic
is proposed to reduce extrapolation-sensitive value estimation
and stabilize policy optimization during distributional transi-
tion. In addition, CL auxiliary mechanisms are incorporated
to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in sequential tasks.

considers continual adaptation and explainability-oriented
policy learning in sequential navigation tasks.
2) A two-stage behavior cloning mechanism is proposed to
balance early-stage knowledge guidance and late-stage
autonomous behavior formation, enabling efficient policy
learning while preserving the emergence of explainable
behavior patterns.
3) A fuzzy critic is proposed to stabilize the transition from

II. RELATED WORK
A. Guided Reinforcement Learning
Exploration inefficiency remains a major challenge in
RL for robotic navigation and control, especially in high-
dimensional or sparse-reward environments. To alleviate this
issue, many studies have introduced external knowledge or
expert experience to guide policy learning and improve sample
efficiency.
One representative direction incorporates expert demonstra-
tions or prior policies into RL. For example, expert mod-
els, hybrid action selection strategies, and offline-generated
trajectories have been employed to constrain the exploration
space and accelerate policy initialization [5]–[7]. In addition,
methods such as Hindsight Experience Replay and BC regu-
larization improve learning stability by encouraging policies
to reuse historically effective behaviors [8]. Some studies
further reduce learning difficulty through task decomposition
and staged policy guidance [9].
Recently, fuzzy systems have also been integrated into RL to
provide structured and explainable guidance mechanisms. By
combining fuzzy-rule reasoning with RL policies, these meth-
ods improve exploration stability while maintaining adaptive
capability in complex environments [10], [11].
Inspired by these studies, this research adopts BC to im-
prove policy learning efficiency in the early training stage.
B. Continual Reinforcement Learning
In long-term robotic navigation, agents are required to
continually adapt to changing environments while preserving
previously acquired knowledge. Existing CRL methods can
generally be divided into three categories: knowledge reuse,
experience replay , and parameter regularization.
Knowledge reuse methods improve cross-task adaptation by
transferring historical policies or representations. Representa-
tive approaches employ policy libraries, attention mechanisms,
or persistent policy guidance to improve transfer efficiency
and reduce forgetting [12], [13]. Experience replay methods
alleviate forgetting by storing and replaying representative ex-
periences, where structured replay and context-aware memory
strategies have shown effectiveness in CL scenarios [2], [14]–
[17]. Parameter regularization methods preserve historical
knowledge by constraining updates to important parameters.
Typical approaches include Fisher-information-based EWC [1]
and trajectory-based SI [18], together with several extended
regularization strategies [19].
Although these approaches have achieved promising results,
most existing studies focus on only a single perspective
and rarely consider CL together with policy guidance and
explainability in a unified framework. Motivated by this obser-
vation, the present work integrates knowledge-guided learning,
continual regularization, and explainable policy structures into
a unified RL framework, thereby enabling both CL capability
and policy explainability.
C. Fuzzy Reinforcement Learning
Due to their explainability and uncertainty modeling capa-
bility, fuzzy systems have been widely incorporated into RL to
improve control performance and training stability. In robotic
control and navigation tasks, fuzzy RL is commonly used for
value approximation, policy guidance, and reward design.
One category of methods employs fuzzy systems as function
approximators or dynamic models to assist RL optimization.
Representative studies utilized fuzzy systems to approximate
value functions, model uncertain dynamics, and construct
approximately optimal controllers for nonlinear systems [20]–
[22]. These approaches improve modeling capability and sys-
tem robustness, but fuzzy systems are generally treated only
as auxiliary components.
Another category directly integrates fuzzy systems into
policy or control architectures. For example, hierarchical
frameworks combined RL decision-making with low-level
fuzzy controllers [23], while several studies fused fuzzy prior
policies with RL policies through adaptive weighting mech-
anisms [24]. Fuzzy systems have also been employed for
reward shaping and auxiliary evaluation in complex control
problems [25]. Although these methods improve explainability
and stability, most adopt loosely coupled designs between
fuzzy systems and RL policies.
Overall, existing fuzzy RL methods mainly utilize fuzzy
systems for auxiliary modeling or external guidance, while
deeper integration with policy learning and CL mechanisms
remains limited. Motivated by this observation, the pro-
posed framework incorporates fuzzy systems directly into the
network structure for value estimation and policy learning,
thereby enabling explainability and CL capability within a
unified framework.
D. Hierarchical Explainability in Reinforcement Learning
As RL has been increasingly applied to robotic control
and autonomous navigation, policy explainability has attracted
growing attention. Existing studies mainly improve explain-
ability from two perspectives: post-hoc explanation and struc-
tural explainability.
Post-hoc methods analyze trained policies to estimate the
contribution of input features to decision-making. Represen-
tative approaches employ Shapley-value-based analysis, se-
mantic feature grouping, and attention mechanisms to explain
policy behaviors from the input–output perspective [3], [26],
[27]. These methods provide intuitive explanation of policy
decisions without modifying the original learning framework.
Another line of research improves explainability through
policy structure design. For example, some studies modeled


3
policies using graph-based structures to explicitly represent de-
cision dependencies [4], while others introduced intermediate
semantic variables or auxiliary tasks to generate explainable
latent representations during decision-making [28]. Compared
with post-hoc analysis, these approaches provide more direct
structural explanation of the policy process.
Overall, existing explainable RL methods have demon-
strated the effectiveness of both external explanation and struc-
tural modeling. Motivated by these studies, the present work
adopts an intermediate-variable-based explainability frame-
work by introducing semantically meaningful latent variables
into the policy architecture, thereby enabling structured expla-
nation of navigation behaviors.
III. METHODOLOGY
A. Overview
The proposed FECHRL framework integrates CRL and
explainable policy learning within a unified architecture. The
overall framework is illustrated in Fig.1. To preserve previ-
ously acquired knowledge during incremental task learning, in-
tegrative consolidation and memory augmentation mechanisms
are incorporated into the learning framework. To improve
policy explainability, a hierarchical actor structure with an
intermediate behavior mode layer is introduced, enabling the
policy generation process to be represented through seman-
tically meaningful latent modes. Furthermore, a fuzzy critic
and a two-stage behavior cloning mechanism are proposed
to support stable policy adaptation during CL. The two-stage
cloning mechanism balances external policy guidance and
autonomous exploration by switching from strong cloning
supervision to weak cloning constraints during training. Mean-
while, the fuzzy critic utilizes controllable initialization and
activation-based local updates to alleviate critic extrapolation
errors during the switching stage, thereby improving learning
stability.
Remark 1: Feature 1: Unified continual and explainable
reinforcement learning. The proposed framework jointly sup-
ports continual task adaptation and semantically explainable
policy generation within a unified learning architecture.
Remark 2: Feature 2: Two-stage guided policy adap-
tation. The proposed two-stage behavior cloning mechanism
balances rapid task adaptation and autonomous policy learn-
ing, enabling explainable behavior mode formation during CL.
Remark 3: Feature 3: Stable fuzzy critic learning. The
fuzzy critic improves training stability during the transition
from imitation learning to autonomous interaction by reducing
critic extrapolation errors.

D. Hierarchical Actor Architecture
This study introduced a hierarchical actor architecture with
intermediate behavioral patterns to establish a semantic asso-
ciation between high-level environmental perception and low-
level motion control.
First, high-dimensional sensory observations obtained from
LiDAR and other perception sensors were mapped into low-
dimensional latent representations through a convolutional
encoding network:
z = fenc(slidar)
(13)
where fenc(·) denotes the convolutional feature extraction
network with parameters W, and z represents the compact
latent representation of the environmental state.
Subsequently, a behavioral pattern generation layer was
introduced to further project the latent features into a low-
dimensional behavioral pattern space:
m = gψ(z)
(14)
where m denotes the high-level behavioral tendency of the
agent under the current state.
Finally, the low-level policy network generated the control
action by jointly considering the latent state representation,
behavioral pattern, and robot state information:
a = πθ(z, m, sbot, sg)
(15)
where πθ(·) denotes the low-level action policy.
By introducing the behavioral pattern variable, the actor
network was able to preserve semantic behavioral information
during the action generation process, thereby improving the
explainability of policy execution and enhancing the structural
decoupling between high-level decision tendencies and low-
level control actions.

1) Network Architecture: The overall network architecture
is illustrated in Fig. 4. The actor network consists of three
layers. The feature extraction layer receives 90-dimensional
point cloud inputs and performs feature compression. The in-
termediate layer generates behavior modes, which are concate-
nated with the 2-dimensional target state and 2-dimensional
action command as the input to the low-level policy network.
The fuzzy critic receives the concatenated state-action vector
and performs inference using 600 fuzzy rules. In addition,
the fuzzy dynamic reward network is implemented as a
fixed-parameter network to generate dynamic reward weights.

V. RESULTS AND ANALYSIS
A. Incremental Task Training Comparison
As environmental complexity gradually increases, the train-
ing process is divided into four sequential navigation tasks.
T1 corresponds to a simple open environment and is trained
for 300 episodes, while the more challenging tasks T2-T4 are
each trained for 1000 episodes. The success rate is adopted as
the primary evaluation metric.
Fig. 5 illustrates the training success rates of different RL
methods, including DDPG, DDPG+EWC, DDPG+SI, CORE-
SET, SCOMMER, and the proposed FECHRL framework. For
fairness, all baseline methods follow the same experimental
settings and training protocols as our previous continual nav-
igation study [29].
Overall, all methods achieve relatively high success rates on
the simple T1. However, as task complexity increases, most
baseline methods exhibit degraded adaptability in subsequent
tasks. Consolidation-based methods can alleviate catastrophic
forgetting to some extent, but the imposed parameter con-
straints reduce policy adaptability under new environmental
distributions. In contrast, SCOMMER demonstrates perfor-
mance improvements during the later stages of T3 and T4,
indicating that its dual-memory mechanism provides relatively
stable knowledge transfer during CL.
The proposed FECHRL framework maintains stable learn-
ing performance across all incremental tasks and rapidly re-
covers policy performance after task transitions. This suggests
that the introduced guided learning mechanism effectively

B. Quantitative Analysis of Sequential Task Performance
To further evaluate the CL capability of the proposed frame-
work, quantitative experiments were conducted over four se-
quential navigation tasks. The baseline CL methods follow the
same evaluation protocol and experimental settings adopted
in our previous continual navigation study [29]. The results
averaged over five random seeds are reported in Table II.
Table II presents the performance of different methods after
sequentially learning tasks T1-T4. Here, MTk denotes the
model obtained after completing the training of task Tk. The
last two columns report the average forgetting rate F Tk and
forward transfer rate FT Tk, respectively.
Overall, the baseline methods exhibit different degrees of
catastrophic forgetting during sequential learning. In particu-
lar, KGDDPG shows severe performance degradation on pre-
viously learned tasks after training subsequent environments,
indicating limited knowledge retention capability. Although
KGEWC and KGSI alleviate forgetting through parameter
consolidation, their performance on earlier tasks still decreases
significantly after later-stage training, suggesting that consoli-
dation alone cannot fully balance stability and adaptability in
continual navigation scenarios.
In contrast, the proposed FECHRL framework demonstrates
strong CL capability across all sequential tasks. As shown
in Table II, FECHRL maintains consistently high success
rates on previously learned tasks even after completing all
subsequent training stages. For example, the success rate on
T2 further improves from 0.88 at MT2 to 0.94 at MT4, while
the performance on T1 remains close to perfect throughout
the entire learning process. Moreover, the forgetting metrics
of FECHRL remain negative across all stages, indicating
that subsequent task learning not only alleviates catastrophic
forgetting but also promotes knowledge refinement on previous
tasks.
FECHRL also achieves competitive forward transfer per-
formance. In particular, the framework obtains forward trans-
fer rates of 0.221 and 0.200 on later tasks, demonstrating
that the learned behavioral representations and guided policy
adaptation mechanism provide effective prior knowledge for
unseen environments. These results indicate that the proposed
framework is capable of simultaneously preserving historical
knowledge and supporting efficient adaptation to new tasks,
thereby achieving stable CRL performance.

switching, were introduced. To further analyze the differences
among these strategies, comparisons are conducted from two
perspectives: training performance, testing results.

by a subsequent recovery in the later stage. This phenomenon
is mainly caused by the earlier switching point of Hard
switching, which leaves more time for autonomous exploration
and interaction. Therefore, the temporary performance decline
is associated with increased exploration capability.

two aspects: behavior mode learning and internal reasoning.

weights provide semantic explanations for policy actions,
while the internal reasoning process of reward generation can
be further visualized through rule activations. The objective of
this analysis is not only to explain the final navigation actions,
but also to verify whether the learned intermediate variables
preserve human-understandable semantic meanings.

VI. CONCLUSION
This study investigated the integration of continual learning
capability and policy explainability in robotic navigation tasks.
Since continual learning prefers strong prior constraints while
explainability-oriented behavior learning requires sufficient
autonomous interaction, a conflict exists between the two
objectives during policy optimization. To address this issue,
an explainability-oriented continual reinforcement learning
framework, FECHRL, was proposed. A two-stage behavior
cloning mechanism was introduced to balance early-stage
knowledge guidance and late-stage autonomous behavior for-
mation, while a localized fuzzy critic was designed to stabilize
the transition from cloning-guided learning to autonomous
interaction learning. Experimental results in sequential nav-
igation tasks demonstrated that the proposed framework can
simultaneously maintain continual learning capability and gen-
erate semantically explainable behavior patterns. In particular,
the proposed two-stage cloning strategy was shown to play an
important role in balancing policy stability and explainability-
oriented behavior learning. Although the proposed method is
effective, it still relies on multiple auxiliary mechanisms and
hierarchical components, resulting in relatively high structural
complexity. Future work will investigate whether similar sus-
tained adaptation and explainability features can be achieved
through more compact policy structures and intrinsic repre-
sentation learning mechanisms.
