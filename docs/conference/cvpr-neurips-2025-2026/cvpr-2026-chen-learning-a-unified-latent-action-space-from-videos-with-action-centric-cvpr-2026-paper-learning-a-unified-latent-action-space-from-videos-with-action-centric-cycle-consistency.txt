# Abstract 

Video data provides a rich source beyond expensive action-labeled data for advancing robot learning. Recent ap-proaches have demonstrated promising potential in leverag-ing video data by learning latent actions for policy training. The latent action tokenizer encodes latent actions between successive video frames, and the tokenizer is trained to recon-struct future frames using current frames and the encoded latent actions. However, the unique pairing of successive frames permits future frame reconstruction with little un-derstanding of transition dynamics, hindering the learning of semantically consistent latent actions. Moreover, the to-kenizer typically allocates distinct latent action subsets to individual embodiments to accommodate heterogeneous mor-phologies, constraining knowledge transfer. To overcome such limitations, we propose the action-centric cycle consis-tency, aiming to establish a unified latent action space. Our method samples latent actions from the latent action space

Our concise method creates a challenging task that learns corresponding latent actions from current frames and diverse generated future frames, compelling the tokenizer to develop semantically consistent action representations. In real-world experiments, our method maintains strong performance with a 44% improvement.

Imitation learning from expert demonstrations has emerged as an effective approach for robotic skill acquisition [2– 4]. However, the process of gathering such demonstrations requires substantial human effort and expertise, thereby cre-ating a significant constraint in advancing robotic skill acqui-This CVPR paper is the Open Access version, provided by the Computer Vision Foundation. Confronted with this challenge, videos emerge as a promising alternative, providing an abundant data repository that captures behavioral patterns and physical dynamics. Prior work has explored various approaches to leverage video data, including visual pre-training [5–11], learning reward functions [12–17]. However, these approaches are limited to static state representations or suffer from sim-to-real transfer gaps. Recent studies [1, 18–21] have attempted to address these limitations by learning latent actions and pre-training policy models on these latent representations. The latent action tokenizer extracts latent actions between successive video frames, with the tokenizer trained to recon-struct subsequent frames from both the latent actions and current frames. Nevertheless, each current frame is uniquely paired with a future frame, which allows the future frame reconstruction with limited understanding of the underly-ing transition dynamics, thereby impeding the extraction of semantically consistent latent actions. Moreover, to ac-commodate heterogeneous robotic morphologies, distinct regions within the latent action space are typically assigned to individual embodiments, which constrains the knowledge transfer across embodiments. To verify our hypotheses, we conduct pilot studies to investigate the learned latent actions from existing methods, such as Genie [1]. We first apply the latent actions encoded from reference video frames to the current frame. As shown in Figure 1(a), the generated frame exhibits divergent motion with the reference video frames, which indicates limitations in the semantic consistency. We then visualize latent action spaces on distinct embodiments in Figure 1(b) and observe fragmentation within these representational spaces, which indicates the lack of cross-embodiment unification. These empirical findings prompt the following question: Can we establish a unified latent action space that achieves both semantic consistency and cross-embodiment unification to enhance video pre-training effectiveness? 

In this work, we propose to learn latent actions with action-centric cycle consistency, aiming to establish a uni-fied latent action space. Specifically, our method samples latent actions from the latent action space and decodes them with video frames to generate subsequent frames. Cycle consistency is then enforced by predicting the sampled latent actions from the video frames and the corresponding gener-ated frames. Unlike previous methods that are limited by the unique pairing between successive video frames, which hin-ders effective latent action learning. Our concise approach creates a challenging task that learns corresponding latent actions from current frames and diverse generated future frames, compelling the tokenizer to develop semantically consistent action representations. Furthermore, our method enables direct cross-embodiment learning by utilizing sam-pled latent actions and video frames from distinct embodi-ments. Cycle consistency enforces alignment between the sampled and predicted latent actions that originate from dif-ferent embodiments, thereby facilitating the unification of the latent action space across embodiments. While conceptually promising, establishing action-centric cycle consistency presents significant challenges. (I) The distributional mismatch between decoder-generated images and those from the dataset compromises the encoder per-formance. Furthermore, the decoder may leak latent action information directly to the encoder through generated frames. To address this issue, we introduce a local-global discrimina-tor that promotes the distribution alignment between gener-ated frames and dataset images across both global style and local details, thereby mitigating distributional discrepancies and effectively preventing information leakage. (II) The la-tent action space changes dynamically during the tokenizer optimization, precluding the establishment of a predefined space suitable for sampling. While sampling from the cur-rent batch offers a straightforward solution, the tokenizer may compress the latent action space to reduce cycle con-sistency difficulty. To address this challenge, we propose a simple yet effective method that accumulates latent actions across multiple preceding batches, thereby approximating the latent action space while preventing its collapse. Based on the preceding analysis, we propose CycleMimic, a novel approach that facilitates policy learning on video data through the Action-Centric Cycle Consistency (AC3) framework. Our approach learns latent action representations from video frames, while simultaneously introducing action-centric cycle-consistency constraints to establish a unified latent action space. Subsequently, we pre-train the policy model to predict these encoded latent action tokens. We then fine-tune the policy using action-labeled data, transferring the knowledge acquired from video data to robotic execution tasks. Experi-mental results demonstrate that AC3 significantly enhances the latent action learning effectiveness, and CycleMimic achieves substantial improvements over baseline methods. Our main contributions can be summarized as follows: (I) We propose CycleMimic, a novel framework that enhances video pre-training effectiveness by establishing a unified latent action space, thereby enabling scalable and efficient policy learning from diverse video datasets. (II) We propose an action-centric cycle consistency mechanism designed to learn a unified latent action space, incorporating a local-global discriminator to address distributional mismatch and a latent action buffer to dynamically approximate the latent action space. (III) Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves a 20.1% improvement over OpenVLA on the LIBERO benchmark and increases the average number of consecutively completed tasks from 3.27 to 3.93 on the CALVIN benchmark. In real-world experiments, our method maintains strong performance with a 44% improvement. 12872 2. Related work 2.1. Learning from Videos for Manipulation 

Learning from video data provides a promising paradigm to alleviate the reliance on expensive action-labeled robot data. To exploit readily available video data resources, nu-merous learning frameworks have been developed for robot learning applications. A plethora of research [9, 10, 22] focuses on learning task-relevant priors from videos to guide robot behaviors or derive heuristic reward functions for rein-forcement learning. However, these methods face significant challenges in bridging the sim-to-real gap and construct-ing task-specific environments. Another research direction [6, 23, 24] investigates leveraging video data to pretrain vi-sual representations for subsequent imitation learning tasks. Nevertheless, these visual representations capture only the state at individual time steps, thus largely overlooking tran-sition dynamics. Recently, research on extracting action representations directly from video sequences has attracted considerable attention [18–20, 25, 26]. These methods learn quantized latent actions from consecutive image frames, pro-viding latent action annotations for policy learning. Genie [1] introduces unsupervised learning of latent actions from large-scale video datasets. Concurrently, LAPA [18] is pre-trained to predict single-step future latent actions, while IGOR [19] employs latent actions as intermediate goals. 

## 2.2. Latent Action Learning 

Previous works have employed latent tokens across diverse scenarios. Play-LMP [27] encodes behavioral patterns from play data into a latent plan space for goal-conditioned exe-cution, VPT [28] uses inverse dynamics models trained on limited labeled datasets to annotate unlabeled online data. Although these methods demonstrate the utility of latent representations, they operate by transforming existing ac-tion labels into latent spaces, limiting their applicability to purely unlabeled video data. To address this limitation, recent approaches [18–20] have shifted toward learning la-tent actions directly from temporal dynamics in video se-quences, eliminating the requirement for action supervision. Genie [1] extracts latent actions via a causal latent action model, conditioning on next-frame prediction. LAPA [18] discretizes inter-frame transitions into quantized latent ac-tions, which subsequently serve as supervision signals for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model pretraining. Sim-ilarly, Moto [20] introduces an unsupervised motion tok-enization framework that transforms video sequences into discrete motion tokens, effectively establishing a motion representation vocabulary from visual data. Although these approaches have demonstrated promising performance, they remain limited in learning semantically consistent latent ac-tion representations. Moreover, the latent action spaces of different embodiments are typically fragmented. 

## 2.3. Learning with Cycle Consistency 

Cycle consistency enforces transitivity constraints between bidirectional mapping functions, proving particularly effec-tive when explicit correspondences between domains are unavailable. This principle has demonstrated strong perfor-mance across multiple computer vision domains. In unpaired image-to-image translation, cycle consistency addresses the lack of explicit correspondence between source and target domains X and Y [29–32]. CycleGAN [29] learns bidirec-tional mappings G : X → Y and F : Y → X , with cycle consistency ensuring that F (G(x)) ≈ x and G(F (y)) ≈ y.Beyond domain translation, cycle consistency has proven effective for establishing correspondences in challenging scenarios. FlowWeb [33] leverages cycle-consistent flow fields to optimize globally-consistent dense correspondences across image collections. The principle extends naturally to temporal domains, where Dwibedi et al. [34] introduce tem-poral cycle-consistency (TCC) to discover correspondences across time in video sequences, enabling video alignment through learned embedding spaces. Additional applications span co-segmentation [35, 36], structure from motion [37, 38], and image matching [33, 39, 40], demonstrating the versatility of cycle-consistency constraints.

Discriminator . The distributional discrepancy between decoder-generated frames and dataset frames degrades en-coder performance. Furthermore, the decoder may leak latent action information directly to the encoder through the generated frame, resulting in correspondences that appear cycle-consistent yet remain fundamentally erroneous. To address this issue, we introduce a local-global discriminator that aligns the distribution of generated frames with dataset frames across both local details and global style. This regu-larization simultaneously prevents information leakage since embedding latent actions into generated frames induces dis-tributional deviations that the discriminator penalizes. Additionally, we apply convolution and global pooling operations to the patch features to derive global fea-tures, from which global logits are obtained. ## 3.3. Action Fine-tuning 

To effectively transfer prior knowledge acquired from action-free video datasets to precise robot execution, we integrate an action decoder with the pre-trained policy, and append the action query tokens ACT following the latent actions for the robot action prediction, then the decoder transforms the predicted action tokens into continuous robot action values. The action decoder receives visual and action embeddings from the final layer of the VLM as input. It then aggregates this information through an attention mechanism and pools the representations into a unified embedding, which is sub-sequently mapped to robot actions via an MLP. The policy is fine-tuned on action-labeled datasets containing ground truth actions represented as delta end-effector movements. During this process, the action decoder receives full parame-ter training, while the pre-trained VLM is fine-tuned using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) [46] to preserve pre-trained knowledge while enabling efficient adaptation to the robotic domain. The complete architecture is trained end-to-end by jointly optimizing the latent action prediction loss and the L1 loss between ground truth and predicted low-level actions.

## 4.5. Ablation Analysis 

Comprehensive ablation studies are conducted to investigate the fundamental designs of our approach. All model variants are pre-trained on the Bridge dataset, followed by fine-tuning on LIBERO. We assess the impact of these design decisions by evaluating success rates on the LIBERO benchmark. 

Latent action buffer . Table 4(a) examines the effect of batch accumulation quantity on latent action buffer con-struction. Single-batch accumulation, which directly utilizes latent actions from the current batch, yields performance deterioration due to latent action space collapse. Conversely, excessive batch accumulation also degrades performance. We analyze that tokenizer updates may discard obsolete latent actions, and incorporating latent actions from exces-sively early batches leads to latent action space contamina-tion, compromising latent action learning efficacy. 

Discriminator architectures . Table 4(b) examines various discriminator architectural configurations. The ablation with-out a discriminator shows a performance decline, indicating that discriminator supervision is essential to prevent infor-mation leakage from decoder to encoder regarding sampled latent actions. The variant utilizing a local discriminator for patch-level supervision also demonstrates performance degradation, validating the efficacy of our proposed local-global discriminator in mitigating information leakage and distribution misalignment. 

Discriminator depth . Table 4(c) analyzes the impact of discriminator depth on model performance. Shallow dis-criminators exhibit reduced performance due to insufficient capacity to maintain equilibrium with the encoder, poten-tially causing latent action information leakage. A 12-layer discriminator, matching the encoder depth, achieves satis-factory performance. Considering the computational cost of deeper architectures, we set the depth of the discriminator to 12 layers as the default configuration. 

Action decoding . We examine three distinct action decod-ing approaches. The first approach discretizes actions into bins and employs next-token prediction for discrete action classification. However, this method suffers from substan-tial performance degradation due to limited representational precision. The second approach decodes latent actions into continuous outputs, demonstrating improved performance relative to discrete action decoding. Our proposed method incorporates action tokens subsequent to latent actions and derives continuous actions from the hidden states of these action tokens, yielding further performance enhancement. We analyze that decoding action values directly from latent action tokens simultaneously subjects these tokens to both continuous action regression and latent action prediction constraints, thereby complicating model optimization. 

# 5. Conclusion 

This paper presents CycleMimic, a novel approach that fa-cilitates policy learning from video data through an Action-Centric Cycle Consistency (AC3) framework. The proposed method samples latent actions from a learned latent action space and combines them with video frames to generate subsequent frames. Cycle consistency is enforced by pre-dicting the sampled actions from both the original video frames and the generated frames. In contrast to previous approaches, where the direct pairing of successive frames simplifies latent action learning and compromises semantic consistency within the latent action space, our method intro-duces a more challenging learning objective that compels the tokenizer to develop semantically coherent action represen-tations. Furthermore, the sampled latent actions are applied across video frames from different embodiments, thereby fa-cilitating cross-embodiment alignment of latent actions. The proposed approach learns a unified latent action space that enables Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) to perform effective pre-training exclusively on video data. Experimen-tal evaluation demonstrates that our approach achieves a 20.1% improvement over OpenVLA on the LIBERO bench-mark and increases the average number of completed tasks from 3.27 to 3.93 on the CALVIN benchmark.
