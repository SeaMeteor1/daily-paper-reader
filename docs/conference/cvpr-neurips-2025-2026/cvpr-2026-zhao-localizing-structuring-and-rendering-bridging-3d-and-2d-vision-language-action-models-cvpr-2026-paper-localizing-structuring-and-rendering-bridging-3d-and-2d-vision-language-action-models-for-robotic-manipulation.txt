Abstract
Robotic manipulation in complex 3D environments requires
unifying spatial reasoning with intuitive visual perception,
which is a capability that current Vision-Language-Action
paradigms address separately. While 3D VLAs excel in ge-
ometric and physical reasoning, they lack intuitive, image-
level understanding and dense visual semantics; conversely,
2D VLAs (even with depth image) provide rich visual intu-
ition and semantic continuity but miss explicit spatial global
grounding. We introduce DiffRender-VLA, a differentiable
rendering–based framework that bridges 3D and 2D Vision-
Language-Action models through gradient-consistent vi-
sual mediation. It generates differentiable images by lo-

1. Introduction
Robotic manipulation in complex 3D environments remains
a central challenge in artificial intelligence and robotics.
Despite advances in computer vision [15], natural language
processing [8], and reinforcement learning [41], robots
still struggle to achieve human-level spatial understand-
ing and visually grounded reasoning.
The key difficulty
lies in coupling geometric reasoning with semantic per-
ception—robots must not only reason about 3D spatial
structures but also interpret visual cues in an interpretable,
image-centric manner.
Recent research in vision-language-action (VLA) mod-
els has advanced along two complementary directions. 3D
spatial VLAs, such as PerAct [39] and VoxPoser [17], em-
phasize precise geometric reasoning through point clouds
or voxels, achieving physically grounded action prediction
but sacrificing visual intuitiveness. Conversely, 2D visual
VLAs, such as RT-2 [4], OpenVLA [22], RoboMamba [2],
and PaLM-E [9], leverage high-level semantics from im-
ages but lack explicit 3D grounding. This dichotomy leaves
a gap: 3D VLAs reason geometrically but fail to “see,”
while 2D VLAs perceive richly but fail to reason spatially.
A natural extension to enhance spatial awareness in 2D
VLAs is to incorporate depth information. Recent studies
such as Depth Helps [37], RoboFlamingo-Plus [26], and
DepthVLA [49] adopt RGB-D fusion to provide richer ge-
ometric cues and improve manipulation accuracy. While
effective to some extent, depth maps remain limited as they
describe only surface geometry from a fixed viewpoint and
lack explicit encoding of object-to-object spatial relation-
ships. Moreover, most fusion strategies operate at the fea-
ture level [38], which improves performance but offers lit-
tle interpretability regarding how spatial cues are integrated.
Thus, despite their benefits, RGB-D approaches still strug-
gle to convey holistic 3D scene understanding in an image-
interpretable and reasoning-compatible manner.
We propose DiffRender-VLA, a differentiable render-
ing–based framework that bridges 3D spatial reasoning and
2D visual perception through continuous visual mediation.
DiffRender-VLA generates differentiable images by first lo-
calizing the next end-effector target and inserting a world-
aligned cube marker into the point cloud, then differentiably
structuring surrounding geometry whose color encodes spa-
tial relations to the marker, and finally rendering adaptive
viewpoints optimized to reveal the spatial configuration be-
tween the target and its environment. Together, these com-
ponents form a closed differentiable loop: localization de-
fines where to act, structuring encodes what relations ex-
ist, and rendering determines how to visualize them. This
design unifies perception and reasoning, achieving substan-
tial performance gains in occluded, cluttered, and spatially
complex manipulation tasks.
Comprehensive experiments in both simulation and
real-world robotic systems validate the effectiveness of
DiffRender-VLA. It achieves up to 11.2% performance
gain under occlusion, 17.8% in cluttered scenes, and 13.4%
in tasks requiring intricate spatial reasoning, consistently
outperforming RGB- and RGB-D-based methods. These
results confirm that differentiable rendering provides an ef-
ficient pathway for transferring 3D spatial understanding
into visually interpretable action policies. Our main con-
tributions are as follows:
• We propose DiffRender-VLA, a unified framework that
bridges 3D spatial reasoning and 2D visual percep-
tion to transfer geometric understanding into image-
interpretable action policies.
• We introduce a three-stage pipeline (localization, struc-
turing, and rendering) that transforms point-cloud geom-
etry into 2D representations embedding spatial semantics.
• We demonstrate that differentiable rendering provides a
closed gradient loop where 2D VLA losses backpropa-
gate into 3D representations.
• We achieve state-of-the-art performance with +12.1%
average improvement over existing methods, validated
across both simulation and real-world robotic systems.
2. Related Work
3D VLA Models. 3D VLA models [5, 21, 24]reason di-
rectly in 3D space through explicit geometric representa-
tions such as voxels or point clouds. PerAct [39] voxelizes
the workspace with Perceiver transformers, C2F-ARM [20]
performs coarse-to-fine voxelization, and Act3D [12] ap-
plies adaptive point sampling.
RVT [13, 14] leverages
multi-view transformers, DP3 [52] and 3D Diffuser Ac-
tor [21] introduce diffusion-based 3D reasoning, while GN-
Factor [51] and ManiGaussian [30] employ neural fields
and Gaussian splatting for continuous 3D understanding.
VoxPoser [17] and Spatial-VLM [6] incorporate language-
grounded 3D reasoning.
These spatial approaches excel
in geometric precision but sacrifice visual intuitiveness and
scalability. Comprehensive reviews [32, 45] emphasize that
bridging 3D reasoning with image-based interpretability re-
mains a major problem.
2D VLA Models.
2D VLA models [7, 25, 27] pre-
dict actions from visual-language observations, leveraging
large-scale pretraining to achieve semantic generalization.
Notable systems include RT-1/RT-2 [4], OpenVLA [22],
RoboMamba [2], PaLM-E [9], and Pi0 [3].
While of-
fering strong visual understanding, these models lack ex-
plicit spatial grounding, leading to errors in fine-grained
manipulation. To enrich spatial cues, several works aug-
ment RGB inputs with depth or multimodal fusion. Depth
Helps [37], RoboFlamingo-Plus [1], and DepthVLA [49]
show that depth improves local geometry perception but
remains view-dependent and feature-level, offering limited
3D interpretability. Video- and latent-action pretraining ap-

3. Method
We present DiffRender-VLA, which, instead of choosing
between image-based and 3D reasoning, enables gradient
flow to transfer the 3D perception capabilities into 2D VLA
models. Figure 2 illustrates the overall framework.

3.3. Rendering Adaptive Viewpoint
Suboptimal viewpoints severely degrade spatial informa-
tion—occlusions hide cube faces, unfavorable angles ob-
scure gradients, or cubes appear too small for meaningful
cues [33]. Our mechanism learns camera positioning that
maximizes spatial visibility in 2D projections.
We learn viewpoint parameters θview (predicted along-
side Qcoarse and Zcoarse by Φenc above) through action-loss
backpropagation. This enables joint reasoning about the tar-
get location and observation perspective as a coupling that
is difficult to achieve through separate optimization.
For each dynamic camera i ∈{1, . . . , Ndyn}, we de-
code valid parameters (Ri, ti, ϕi) = Dview(θi) where Ri ∈
SO(3) is rotation, ti ∈R3 is translation, and ϕi ∈R+
is field-of-view. Decoder Dview ensures plausible configu-
rations through bounded transformations and a 6D rotation
representation [56] for stable gradients.
By learning viewpoints end-to-end through task loss, the
model discovers task-relevant perspectives: clearly reveal-
ing color gradients from multiple faces (maximizing di-
rectional information), minimizing occlusions of the target
end-effector region (preserving spatial context), and adjust-
ing field-of-view for sufficient cube size (enhancing per-
ceptibility).

Table 4 evaluates zero-shot transfer to out-of-distribution

indicating world-aligned beam encoding provides more ro-
bust appearance-invariant spatial features.
5. Conclusion
DiffRender-VLA bridges 3D and 2D VLA paradigms
through differentiable rendering, enabling gradient flow to
transfer spatial 3D perception into interpretable image rep-
resentations. Our pipeline—coarse cube prediction, spatial
beam markers, adaptive viewpoint generation—channels
spatial understanding into image-based manipulation with-
out requiring explicit 3D inputs or abandoning visual inter-
pretability. By rendering axis-aligned colored beams that
encode directional distances, we create visual semantics in-
terpretable by standard vision encoders while preserving 3D
geometric precision. Experiments demonstrate 80.5% sim-
ulation success and 78.3% real-world success.
